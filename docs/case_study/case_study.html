1. Start with the business, not the data

Who will use the dashboard, and what decision does each person own? OpenRouter is a two-sided marketplace, like Olist: labs supply models, apps buy tokens. The stakeholders and questions below are my own design.

StakeholderDecision they ownQuestion the dashboard must answer
Leadership / strategyWhich labs to partner with or bet onWho is winning tokens, and is the market concentrating or spreading out?
Pricing / financeHow to price, and where the money isDoes the cheapest model win? How does a lab's token share compare with its share of spend?
AI platform teamWhich model to route each workload toWhich models give the best quality for the price?
Competitive intelligenceWhere the threats come fromAre open-weight models and Chinese labs gaining on closed US models?
Product / GTMLaunch planning; which apps and tasks to courtDo new models keep their usage after launch? Which apps and tasks drive demand?

Why this subject. I coded 221 analyst postings from 58 tech companies (my own job-search scans, Oct 9-19, 2026). With company boilerplate removed, 43% ask analysts to use AI tools in the work, 15% ask them to analyze AI itself (product usage, AI cost, model evaluation), and Tableau appears in 25% against Power BI's 8%. A real-data answer to "who is winning AI usage?" speaks to that demand directly.

Analyst tip. Phrase each question as a decision: "Should we route bulk jobs to a cheaper model?" beats "show model usage". It tells you which chart wins.

2. Understand the data before trusting it

Pull everything once, keep each response whole with its as_of stamp, then profile it: row counts, keys, date ranges, gaps, odd values.

SourceWhat it holdsHistory
Rankings (daily)Top 50 models per day by tokens, plus one "other" rowFrom 2025-01-01
Rankings by category (monthly)Top models within 12 task categories (sampled)From 2025-05
App rankings (monthly)Top 100 apps by tokens and requestsFrom 2025-01
BenchmarksArtificial Analysis indices, Design Arena, OpenRouter evalsSnapshot
Model list + 22 Internet Archive snapshotsPrices, release dates, Hugging Face links (open weights)Monthly, Jan 2025 to Oct 2026

51 API calls, capped and stopped at the first error. Eight findings shaped every later step:

  • Explosive growth. Monthly tokens grew 258-fold (2.2T to 575.9T, Jan 2025 to Sep 2026), so an "all-time share" is really the last few months. Shares are always shown per month.
  • Two missing days (2025-06-15 and 2025-07-15) stay gaps, never zeros; monthly volume is shown as average tokens per day.
  • One model, many IDs. Of 436 slugs, 166 carry a release-date suffix and 80 are :free variants, so each slug rolls up to a model family and a lab.
  • Unattributable tokens. The long tail outside each day's top 50 is 6.6% of tokens; unnamed stealth models are 4.8% of named tokens. Both are their own groups, never guessed.
  • Prices change and models retire. Today's price list covers 88.2% of named tokens; adding the monthly Archive snapshots raises that to 97.7%.
  • Partial edge buckets. Each yearly category pull also returned a partial month before its start date (about 12% of the real value). Twelve were dropped.
  • Categories are samples summing to 85-91% of tokens, so they are used as mix shares, not totals.
  • Benchmarks are a snapshot covering 69% of the last 90 days' named tokens, so the quality chart uses that window and says so.
Analyst tip. An API pull is a snapshot. Store the response's own timestamp with every number you publish: OpenRouter notes its figures can be restated.

3. Model the data and agree the definitions

A small star schema in DuckDB. Facts hold what you add up; dimensions hold what you filter by. Every foreign key is checked.

Metric dictionary. Each metric is defined once and reused by both tools.

MetricDefinitionDecision behind it
Token shareTokens / all tokens that period, including the long tailA share of named tokens would inflate every lab
Blended price15/16 input price + 1/16 output priceOpenRouter's own 100T-token study reports about 6K prompt vs 400 completion tokens per request; 3:1 kept as a check
Estimated spendTokens × blended list priceBefore caching discounts, so an upper bound: used for shares, never dollar totals
Lab HHI10,000 × sum of squared lab shares of named tokensRead against the DOJ/FTC 2023 line: above 1,800 is "highly concentrated"
Launch curveA model family's daily share by week since it entered the top 50Only families first seen on or after 2025-02-01, so a launch is a real entry
CountryThe developer's stated headquartersNot where a model is hosted, and not a base model's origin

16 integrity checks run after every build: tokens reconcile exactly to the raw pulls (2,368,187,299,116,173), shares sum to 100% every month, no orphan keys, no conflicting prices per snapshot.

Spot-check against the public page. The live openrouter.ai/rankings leaderboard (Today, This Week, This Month top 10s) was rebuilt from one extra API call: 29 of 30 rows match on model and rounded tokens. The one miss (Step 5 Preview, 8.21T vs 8.22T) is the top-50 cut: the model's last three days fell into "other". The two pulls also agreed on all 306 overlapping rows, so nothing was restated.

Analyst tip. Two calculations agreeing is not proof when they share an assumption. The price bug lived in both sides; they disagreed only because they broke a tie differently. January 2025 had agreed while both were wrong.

5. Explore, then decide what is important

Each hypothesis was tested with several cuts. A finding makes the story only if it is big, reliable across cuts (including the 3:1 price blend) and actionable.

HypothesisWhat the data saidVerdict
Chinese models now lead tokensChina first passed the US the week of Mar 23, 2026 and has led every week since Apr 27. It holds if every unattributed token were American (58.5% vs 40.3%) and without DeepSeek (47.2% vs 36.8%). It fails in March 2026 under every cut.Kept: the hook. By requests it is a tie (44.3% vs 45.0%, one week)
Tokens and spend tell different storiesSep 2026: China 58.5% of tokens but 39.7% of spend; Anthropic 3.4% of tokens but 28.3% of spend; DeepSeek 21.5% of tokens but 5.3% of spend. The gap was at least 18.8 points every month of 2026.Kept: the central finding (the gap, not the level)
Cheap models win volume, premium wins spendModels under $0.25/M carry 59.7% of tokens but 6.2% of spend. The average token got 67% cheaper; the same models' list prices fell 10%, so about nine-tenths of the drop is mix.Kept
New models peak, then fade103 notable launches: by week 12 a model keeps 21% of its peak share, but its lab keeps 70%. US labs keep 72%, Chinese labs 49%.Kept, reframed: models churn, labs retain
The market is spreading outLab HHI fell from 2,855 (Q1 2025) to 1,110 (Q2 2026) and has been below 1,800 every month since Dec 2025, but rose again in Q3 2026.Kept, with the reversal stated
Top scorers aren't the most usedThe 5 highest-scoring models took 2.0% of tokens in 90 days; the 5 most-used (scores 24.4-41.8) took 32.5%. Rank correlation 0.19.Kept, framed as routing

Guarding against over-claiming, written into the story rather than hidden:

  • OpenRouter is not the market. Direct API traffic to OpenAI, Anthropic or Google is invisible here; Menlo Ventures' late-2025 enterprise survey puts Anthropic at 40% of enterprise LLM API spend. Two markets, both real.
  • Tokens are not requests, and requests are not users. The lead holds on tokens; by requests it is a tie.
  • Estimated spend is an upper bound (list price, before caching). It is used for shares only.
  • Benchmarks score one setting per model, while usage counts every setting; cheap models can be the right choice for bulk jobs like classification, the largest task by usage share in the 7-day task snapshot.

6. Choosing each chart, the way a tech company would

Rule of thumb: the job decides the chart. Every chart gets one message, and the title states it with a verified number.

QuestionChart chosenWhy this, and what was rejected
Who is winning, over time?Weekly share lines: China, US, everyone elseA stacked area was rejected by the color validator (gray next to aqua: normal-vision ΔE 14.7, floor 15)
Tokens vs spend, by country and labDumbbell / paired bars on one % axisSame unit, so one axis; a dual-axis chart was ruled out
Did prices fall, or did mix shift?Two indexed lines, Jan 2025 = 100Indexing puts both on one scale instead of two axes
Quality for the priceBubble scatter, log price axisPrices span $0.06-$75/M; a linear axis crams the cheap models into a corner
Is it one lab or many?Small multiples, shared y-axisSix lines on one chart would need six hues

Style rules, sourced to tech companies' own design systems: summary first and one chart per panel (GitLab Pajamas, dashboards); categorical colors in a strict fixed order (IBM Carbon), so China is blue and the US orange on every page; chart color on data marks only, and fade the rest for emphasis (Atlassian). Non-country charts are monochrome: violet for the measure the title is about, gray for its comparison. Every palette was run through a colorblind and contrast validator in light and dark.

7. Building it in Power BI

Steps, in the order you would do them in the Power BI service (here on a Mac, with a Free license, so no Desktop):

  • Get data: one seed table through Power Query Online ("Text/CSV, link to file"), then the full model pasted into the TMDL view: 15 tables with typed queries reading the project's CSVs from GitHub, so a refresh needs no gateway.
  • Model view: 6 single-direction, many-to-one relationships; months reach the daily fact through dates.
  • Measures, not columns: 23 DAX measures from one measures.dax, including lab HHI as a SUMX over labs and spend share with REMOVEFILTERS.
  • Reconcile: one DAX query returning 22 headline numbers; all 22 match the pandas answer key to 9 significant digits.
  • Pages: one per stakeholder, KPI row on top; the report is generated as PBIR files and published with the Fabric REST API.

Problems the live build found, each fixed and re-checked: a measure named Tokens collided with the tokens column (DAX names ignore case); the log axis and the small-multiples grid needed Power BI's real property names, read back after setting them once in the editor; chart titles fell back to a serif font until the theme used web-font stacks; and redefining a date column broke Power BI's hidden date table, so model patches now add columns only.

8. Building it in Tableau, as a Story

Design before you build. All five pages were mocked up in HTML with the real numbers and approved before any Tableau work. Changing a mock takes minutes; re-laying a dashboard takes an hour.

  • One sheet per chart (13 sheets), each reading one flat CSV exported from the same data the mockup used, so titles and KPI tiles cannot drift.
  • Format in the house style: Tableau Book and Semibold, hairline solid gridlines, no borders or bands, card-style dashboard zones, fixed palettes per entity.
  • Story: five story points, one per stakeholder, each caption the finding itself.
  • Generated as code: the workbook XML is written by a Python generator extended from the Olist project, using only formatting attributes found in Tableau-saved workbooks.
  • Checked through Tableau's own log: opening the workbook in Tableau Desktop showed every sheet, dashboard and story point parsed and queried with no errors, after one fix (two manual sorts on text months failed; the months became real dates).

9. Telling the story

A dashboard shows data; a story makes an argument. The story points follow an arc: hook, the twist, the mechanism, the threat, the lever.

Story pointRole in the argumentCaption (the one thing to remember)
1 LeadershipHook: the center of gravity movedChinese labs have led OpenRouter tokens every week since April 27
2 Pricing & financeTwist: volume isn't moneyChina wins the tokens; US labs still win the money
3 AI platformMechanism: routing follows cost, not scoreThe most-used models are cheap mid-scorers
4 Competitive intelThreat: it's broad, not one labChina's lead holds without DeepSeek and under the worst-case count
5 Product & GTMLever: continuity keeps usersModels churn within weeks; labs keep most of their users

Recommendations (my analysis, drawn from the findings):

  • Track tokens and spend side by side. On open marketplaces Chinese labs carry the volume (58.5%) while US labs keep the spend (60.3%); a token-only view would misread the market.
  • Forecast on mix, not list prices. The average token got 67% cheaper while the same models got 10% cheaper; revenue plans should model the shift to cheaper tiers.
  • Route by task, benchmark the frontier first. Bulk work runs on cheap mid-scorers; the 7 price-quality frontier models are the short list to test.
  • Make the upgrade path the retention plan. A model keeps 21% of its peak by week 12 but its lab keeps 70%; US labs hold 72% against 49% for Chinese labs.
  • Watch the bench, not one lab. Five Chinese labs each carry 2%+ of tokens, and the lead survives without DeepSeek.

10. How the story was checked

Five layers of checks, from numbers to narrative. Each one catches a different kind of mistake.

LayerWhat you checkHow
1 NumbersEvery KPI and chart total equals the answer keyPandas answer key 42/42; live Power BI DAX 22/22; public-page spot-check 29/30
2 DefinitionsThe same metric means the same thing everywhereOne metric dictionary; Tableau and Power BI read the same exported numbers
3 Title claimsEvery number in a title or caption is trueTitles are computed from the verified export, not typed; prose numbers re-checked against the data
4 SanityParts add up; nothing silently dropped16 integrity checks: exact token reconciliation, shares sum to 100%, no orphan keys
5 RenderWhat the viewer sees matches the dataEvery Power BI page inspected on screen; Tableau load checked through its own log

Checking caught these before publishing: the variant-price bug (spend share 45.4% → 39.7%); lab retention for Chinese labs overstated (59% → 49%) because labs that left the top 50 had been skipped instead of counted as zero; a crossover date stated as July 6 that was really April 27; a price-tier share added from rounded parts (59.8% → 59.7%); and a "below 1,800 since Q4 2025" claim that was really "since December 2025".

Questions to ask a reviewer before publishing: would a stakeholder reach a different conclusion from the same chart? Is any claim causal that the data only shows as a correlation? What would make the main finding wrong (direct API traffic, caching discounts, stealth models)?

The standard. A number goes on a slide only if it reconciles to the answer key, its definition is written down, and someone other than the builder could recompute it.

11. Limitations and next steps

  • One marketplace. OpenRouter traffic only; enterprise contracts and direct API use are not in the data.
  • Top 50 per day. Models near the cutoff are slightly undercounted; the "other" row bounds the gap.
  • List prices. Estimated spend ignores caching, batch and negotiated discounts, so only shares are reported.
  • Benchmarks are a snapshot of one setting per model; stealth models have no lab; country is the developer's HQ.
  • Next: a scheduled daily pull so both dashboards refresh themselves; cache-aware spend if cache rates become available; and an enterprise-spend comparison to put the two markets side by side.
